The Chain Rule · See the Layers

Composite functions have layers. Read from outside to inside, differentiate one layer at a time, and never lose the derivative of the inside.

Use Mr. Azzam’s inside/outside method for composite functions, powers, radicals, trigonometric compositions, and mixed differentiation rules.

Key takeaway

Mr. Azzam’s Chain Rule routine

1 · Read the layers

Identify the outside operation and complete inside expression.

2 · Keep the inside

Differentiate the outside while leaving the inside unchanged.

3 · Differentiate inside

Find the derivative of the inner function.

4 · Multiply + check

Multiply and check that no inside derivative is missing.

See the layers before you differentiate

The Chain Rule is for a function inside another function. Before differentiating, identify what is outside and what is inside.

Chain Rule can live inside another rule

⌄

First identify the structure of the whole expression. Product or Quotient Rule may organize the outside problem while the Chain Rule is used inside one of its factors.

Product Rule + Chain Rule
y=5x3sec⁡(3x2+x)y=5x^3\sec(3x^2+x)y=5x3sec(3x2+x)y′=15x2sec⁡(3x2+x)+5x3[sec⁡(3x2+x)tan⁡(3x2+x)](6x+1)y'=15x^2\sec(3x^2+x)+5x^3[\sec(3x^2+x)\tan(3x^2+x)](6x+1)y′=15x2sec(3x2+x)+5x3[sec(3x2+x)tan(3x2+x)](6x+1)
Common mistake. Do not apply one rule blindly to the whole expression. Read the structure first: product, quotient, then the composite layers inside.
Key takeawayExam strategy

Choose the outermost structure first. If the whole expression is a product or quotient, organize that rule first, then use Chain Rule inside the composite factors.
