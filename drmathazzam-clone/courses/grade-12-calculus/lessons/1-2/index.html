Finding Limits Graphically and Numerically

Estimate limits from tables and graphs, and understand when a limit fails to exist.

Choose a section to read or explore.

A guide to this lesson
ExplanationWorked exampleInteractive labPracticeCommon mistakeKey takeaway

Lesson overview

⌄

A limit answers one question: where is the function heading? In this lesson, you will answer using two kinds of evidence — a table of nearby values and the graph itself. The value exactly at the target may agree with the limit, differ from it, or not exist at all.

Explanation
Learning objectives
  • Interpret lim⁡x→af(x)=L\lim_{x\to a}f(x)=Llimx→a​f(x)=L as nearby behavior.
  • Estimate a limit numerically using values approaching from both sides.
  • Estimate left-hand, right-hand, and two-sided limits from a graph.
  • Distinguish f(a)f(a)f(a) from lim⁡x→af(x)\lim_{x\to a}f(x)limx→a​f(x).
  • Recognize when a two-sided limit does not exist and explain why.
Definition

Limit

lim⁡x→af(x)=L\lim_{x\to a}f(x)=Llimx→a​f(x)=L means that the outputs f(x)f(x)f(x) get closer to LLL as inputs xxx get closer to aaa from both sides.

Look near the point — not only at the point

⌄

The word approaches is the heart of a limit. Imagine walking toward a doorway: you can tell where you are heading before you stand exactly on the threshold.

When does a limit not exist?

⌄

Before writing DNE, identify the reason. In this lesson there are three important patterns.

Explanation

1 · Jump

Left and right approach different finite values.

Explanation

2 · Unbounded behavior

Outputs grow without bound near a vertical asymptote.

Work it out. f(0.99)=−100f(0.99)=-100f(0.99)=−100 and f(1.01)=100f(1.01)=100f(1.01)=100. Moving closer to 111 makes the magnitudes grow, with opposite signs.

Explanation

3 · Oscillation

Outputs keep changing and never settle to one destination.

Work it out. The outputs stay in [−1,1][-1,1][−1,1], but peaks and troughs keep occurring closer to 000. Bounded does not mean settled.

Key takeaway
Decision routine
1. Find the left-hand behavior.
2. Find the right-hand behavior.
3. If both approach the same finite number, that is the limit.
4. Otherwise state DNE for a finite real limit and name the reason.

This routine checks for a finite real limit. Infinite-limit notation describes unbounded behavior, not a finite output. For this reciprocal, the two sides have opposite signs, so there is no single two-sided destination.
