About this rebuild

From Flash projectors to the open web

The original folder held 31 items — ClickOnce manifests, Flash 5/6 projector executables and a handful of Excel workbooks — for a set of classroom maths tools. Flash support ended in December 2020, the installers are 32-bit Windows only, and several files were duplicates or setup packages. Everything with real maths content in it has been reimplemented from scratch.

What changed

  • Same activities, new engine. Each program was rebuilt around its actual behaviour — the ranges, the operations, the scoring, the printouts — in plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript.
  • Works everywhere. Phones, tablets, Chromebooks, interactive whiteboards. No downloads, no plugins, no accounts.
  • Printable. Worksheets, times tables and reference tables have print stylesheets and fit on A4.
  • Private by default. Scores and settings are stored in this browser's local storage only. Nothing is sent anywhere; there is no analytics or tracking of any kind.
  • Accessible. Keyboard control throughout, visible focus states, reduced-motion support and no reliance on emoji or icon fonts.

Question Studio — every question in one deck

The project's questions lived in four unrelated places. They are now merged into a single searchable studio (#/questions) with 4,741 questions:

SourceQuestionsWhat it gives you
TestGen .BOK (2 books)4,128Precalculus and Calculus wording recovered by tools/parse_bok.py — the gaps are the formula objects still held in ExamView's internal notation, as in the .bnk recovery.
.bnk banks (31 chapters)2,840Recovered wording from the Quiz Show banks. Generated numbers show as ruled gaps you can click and fill with your own — no answer key exists in the file.

Calculus question bank162Limits, continuity, derivative rules, related rates, implicit differentiation — with stored answer keys, hints, per-option feedback and explanations.
Lesson quizzes3Quiz checkpoints lifted straight out of the Grade 12 course pages, with their correct answers.

Each card is live: pick with A–D or 1–4, check with Enter, hint with H, save with S, move with ←/→. Answer-keyed questions mark themselves and explain every option; the recovered and FikrCounts questions lock your choice in and ask you to self-report, because no key survived. Cards can be starred, copied, read aloud, and filtered by source, chapter, level, skill or “ones I got wrong”. Practice runs 10, 20 or 50 of them for score, with a streak, a timer and a review sheet at the end.

Question generator — the TestGen logic, rebuilt

A second Drive folder (test gen) held the machinery behind the question banks: the Savvas enVision ExamView .bnk files for Algebra 1, Algebra 2 and Geometry, plus the TestGen 7.6.5 Windows installer. Those dynamic items do not store finished questions — they store a recipe: named variables with ranges, a constraint expression, a template for the stem and options, and an answer computed from the variables. That idea is now in the site itself (#/generator): 112 recipes across seven courses write a fresh question — options, key, hint and worked steps — on every ask, entirely in the browser. The workbook side is a worksheet builder (10, 20 or 40 questions) with a printable answer key, so the same sheet can be handed out and marked without any software.

Run or host it yourself

The whole site is static: open index.html, or serve the folder (node server.js on port 3000). A vercel.json is included for one-command deploys; no build step and no framework preset is needed.
