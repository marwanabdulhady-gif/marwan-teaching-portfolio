Differentiation Rules · Build the Toolbox

Keep the meaning of derivative from Lesson 2.1, but replace repeated limit work with efficient rules. Read the structure, rewrite when useful, choose the correct rule, then differentiate and interpret.

Build fluency with the basic differentiation rules, trigonometric derivatives, Product and Quotient Rules, higher-order derivatives, and motion applications.

For y=x2sin⁡xy=x^2\sin xy=x2sinx, which pair identifies the two factors?

  1. x2x^2x2 and sin⁡x\sin xsinx
  2. 2x2x2x and cos⁡x\cos xcosx
  3. x2x^2x2 and cos⁡x\cos xcosx

Answer: x2x^2x2 and sin⁡x\sin xsinx

The displayed product is x squared times sine x. Its factors are not their derivatives.

1. Differentiate one factor at a time

y′=(2x)(sin⁡x)+(x2)(cos⁡x)y'=(2x)(\sin x)+(x^2)(\cos x)y′=(2x)(sinx)+(x2)(cosx)

2. Combine the two contributions

y′=2xsin⁡x+x2cos⁡xy'=2x\sin x+x^2\cos xy′=2xsinx+x2cosx
Key takeawayStructure first

Label the two factors before differentiating. Product Rule keeps both derivative contributions; differentiating only one factor is incomplete.

Higher-order derivatives

⌄

A derivative is itself a function, so it can be differentiated again.

f(x)⟶f′(x)⟶f′′(x)⟶f′′′(x)⟶f(4)(x)⟶⋯f(x)\longrightarrow f'(x)\longrightarrow f''(x)\longrightarrow f'''(x)\longrightarrow f^{(4)}(x)\longrightarrow\cdotsf(x)⟶f′(x)⟶f′′(x)⟶f′′′(x)⟶f(4)(x)⟶⋯
ExplanationHigher-order notation

Prime notation is normally used for the first three derivatives. Starting with the fourth derivative, write the order in parentheses rather than adding more prime marks.

f(4)(x)not f′′′′(x)f^{(4)}(x)\quad\text{not }f''''(x)f(4)(x)not f′′′′(x)f(n)(x)for the nth derivativef^{(n)}(x)\quad\text{for the }n\text{th derivative}f(n)(x)for the nth derivative

Rates of change & motion

⌄

The derivative measures rate of change. For straight-line motion, the derivative of position is velocity; the derivative of velocity is acceleration.

v(t)=s′(t),a(t)=v′(t)=s′′(t),speed=∣v(t)∣v(t)=s'(t),\qquad a(t)=v'(t)=s''(t),\qquad \text{speed}=|v(t)|v(t)=s′(t),a(t)=v′(t)=s′′(t),speed=∣v(t)∣
Common mistake. Velocity can be negative, zero, or positive. Speed is the absolute value of velocity and cannot be negative.

Exam strategy & common mistakes

⌄
Key takeaway1 · Look at the structure before differentiating.

Can you rewrite it as powers? Is it a sum, product, quotient, or trig function?

Key takeaway2 · Choose the rule, then differentiate.

Do not use Product or Quotient Rule just because you see multiplication or a fraction if algebra can simplify the expression first.

Key takeaway3 · Simplify the derivative.

Combine like terms and, when appropriate, remove negative exponents from the final form.

Common mistake. Do not use the Chain Rule yet. Composite-function differentiation belongs to Lesson 2.4.
