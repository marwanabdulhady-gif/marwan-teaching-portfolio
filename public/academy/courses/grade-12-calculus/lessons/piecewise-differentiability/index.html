Piecewise Differentiability · Check the Join

At a breaking point, differentiability is a two-gate test: the pieces must meet first, then their slopes must match.

Key takeaway

Mr. Marwan’s two-gate test

Gate 1 · Do the pieces meet?

Check continuity at the breaking point. If continuity fails, stop.

Gate 2 · Do the slopes match?

Compare the left-hand and right-hand derivatives. Equal finite slopes mean differentiable.

What does differentiable mean?

⌄
Definition

Differentiable at x = c

A function is differentiable at x=cx=cx=c when its derivative exists there. At a piecewise breaking point, we must inspect the behavior from both sides.

f−′(c)=f+′(c)⟹f′(c) existsf'_{-}(c)=f'_{+}(c)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad f'(c)\text{ exists}f−′​(c)=f+′​(c)⟹f′(c) exists
Key takeawayThe relationship you must remember
Differentiable at c  ⟹  Continuous at c\text{Differentiable at }c\;\Longrightarrow\;\text{Continuous at }cDifferentiable at c⟹Continuous at cContinuous at c  ⟹̸  Differentiable at c\text{Continuous at }c\;\not\Longrightarrow\;\text{Differentiable at }cContinuous at c⟹Differentiable at c
Common mistake. Do not say “continuous, therefore differentiable.” A corner can be continuous but still have different left and right slopes.
Key takeawayLogic check

Differentiability is stronger than continuity: differentiable implies continuous, but a continuous graph can still fail differentiability at a corner, cusp, or vertical tangent.

Key takeawayWhere should you spend your work?

For a piecewise function, ordinary points use the derivative of the active piece. The joining point is where continuity and both one-sided derivatives must be checked.

When differentiability fails

⌄
Jump discontinuity
ExplanationDiscontinuity

A jump, hole, or other discontinuity automatically means the function is not differentiable there.

Corner / sharp turn
ExplanationCorner / sharp turn

The function is continuous, but the one-sided derivatives are different.

∣x∣:f−′(0)=−1,f+′(0)=1|x|:\qquad f'_{-}(0)=-1,\qquad f'_{+}(0)=1∣x∣:f−′​(0)=−1,f+′​(0)=1
Cusp
ExplanationCusp

The one-sided slopes become unbounded in opposite directions, so the derivative does not exist.

Vertical tangent
ExplanationVertical tangent

The graph is continuous, but the slope becomes infinite. Therefore the ordinary finite derivative does not exist at that point.
