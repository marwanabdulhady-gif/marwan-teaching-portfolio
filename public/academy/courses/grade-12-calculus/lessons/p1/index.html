Graphs and Models

Sketch and interpret graphs of equations, find intercepts, test for symmetry, and determine points of intersection between curves.

Choose a section to read or explore.

Lesson overview

⌄

Graphs turn equations into pictures. In this lesson you'll learn to sketch the graph of an equation, read its key features, and use those features to reason about the equation itself — the visual foundation for everything that follows in calculus.

Explanation
Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson you should be able to:

  • Sketch the graph of an equation using point-plotting and key features.
  • Find xxx- and yyy-intercepts algebraically and locate them on a graph.
  • Test an equation for symmetry about the xxx-axis, yyy-axis, and origin.
  • Find points of intersection of two graphs and interpret them visually.
  • Recognize how equations model real data and predict new values.

The graph of an equation

⌄

The graph of an equation in xxx and yyy is the set of all points (x,y)(x, y)(x,y) whose coordinates satisfy the equation. To sketch by point-plotting, choose several values of xxx, compute yyy, plot the points, and connect them with a smooth curve.

y=x3−4xy = x^3 - 4xy=x3−4x

Drag the graph to pan and scroll to zoom. Notice where the curve rises, falls, and crosses the axes — those crossings are the intercepts we study next.

Intercepts of a graph

⌄

An xxx-intercept is a point (a,0)(a, 0)(a,0) where the graph meets the xxx-axis; a yyy-intercept is a point (0,b)(0, b)(0,b) where it meets the yyy-axis. To find them, set the other variable to zero and solve.

Worked example · Find every intercept

y=x3−4xy=x^3-4xy=x3−4x

Predict the zeros, then reveal the algebra behind the marked graph.

Symmetry of a graph

⌄

Symmetry lets you sketch half a curve and mirror the rest. Test it algebraically:

  • y-axis: replacing xxx with −x-x−x yields an equivalent equation.
  • x-axis: replacing yyy with −y-y−y yields an equivalent equation.
  • Origin: replacing both xxx with −x-x−x and yyy with −y-y−y yields an equivalent equation.
Worked example
Test y=x3−4xy = x^3 - 4xy=x3−4x for origin symmetry. Replace x→−xx \to -xx→−x, y→−yy \to -yy→−y: −y=(−x)3−4(−x)=−x3+4x-y = (-x)^3 - 4(-x) = -x^3 + 4x−y=(−x)3−4(−x)=−x3+4x, so y=x3−4xy = x^3 - 4xy=x3−4x — the original equation. The graph is symmetric about the origin.
  • y = x³ − 4x

Points of intersection

⌄

A point of intersection of two graphs satisfies both equations at once. Find them by solving the equations simultaneously — then confirm visually where the curves cross.

Worked example · Solve both equations together

Mathematical models

⌄

Equations often model real data. A well-chosen model both fits observed points and predicts new ones. The same tools — intercepts, symmetry, and intersections — let you interpret what a model says: where a quantity is zero, how it behaves under reflection, and where two models agree. This is the central reason graphs matter in calculus.
