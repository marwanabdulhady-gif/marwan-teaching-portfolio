Understand → Recognise → Solve efficiently

Start by deciding whether the problem describes a part-to-part ratio, a rate per unit, or two agents working together. Avoid unnecessary algebra by using a common multiplier.

Core idea · Ratios represent parts

A:B=3:5⟹A=3k, B=5kA:B=3:5\quad\Longrightarrow\quad A=3k,\ B=5kA:B=3:5⟹A=3k, B=5k

Worked example · Split a known total

Question: The ratio of junior to senior staff is 3:5. If the company has 96 staff members, how many are junior and how many are senior?

3k+5k=96⟹k=12⟹A=36, B=603k+5k=96\Longrightarrow k=12\Longrightarrow A=36,\ B=603k+5k=96⟹k=12⟹A=36, B=60

GMAT First Move

Translate the relationship: are you comparing parts, totals, speeds, or rates of work?

Faster Method

For a ratio a:b, write ak and bk. For combined work, add fractions of a task completed per hour.

Common Traps

  • Treating 3:5 as 3 out of 5 rather than 3 out of 8 total parts.
  • Adding work completion times rather than rates.
  • Mixing hours and minutes in a unit-rate question.
  • Changing both sides of a ratio when only one amount changes.
