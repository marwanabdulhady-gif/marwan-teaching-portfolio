Think Like a GMAT Test-Taker

Before calculating, inspect denominators, convert units consistently, and look for a faster comparison or simplification.

Faster Methods

Compare fractions by cross-multiplication only when denominators are positive.

Cancel common factors before multiplying fractions.

Convert repeating decimals into rational numbers using an equation.

When fractions have an awkward denominator, compare them with one-half or another familiar benchmark.

For algebraic-looking relative quantities, consider substituting smart values that preserve every constraint.

Treat a fraction bar as parentheses around the entire numerator and the entire denominator.

Worked example 1

Evaluate 43​+65​.

43​+65​=129​+1210​=1219​

Use the least common denominator 12.

Worked example 2

Compare 127​ and 95​.

7⋅9=63>60=5⋅12

Cross-products show 7/12 is larger.

Worked example 3

Calculate (158​)×(7235​) without large multiplication.

158​⋅7235​=3⋅91⋅7​=277​

Cancel across numerator and denominator before multiplication: 8 with 72, and 35 with 15.

Worked example 4

Convert 0.272727... into a fraction.

x=0.27,100x−x=27,x=113​

Multiplying by 100 aligns the repeating block; subtract to eliminate the repeating tail.

Worked example 5

Estimate whether 10149​ is above or below one-half.

2(49)=98<101⟹10149​<21​

Compare to the benchmark 1/2 without long division.

Worked example 6

Why is a/(b+c) not generally equal to a/b+a/c?

2+46​=1,26​+46​=4.5

A denominator sum is one entire grouping; never distribute division over a sum.

Common Traps

  • Do not add denominators when adding fractions.
  • A negative denominator changes the direction of a comparison after multiplication.
  • Finite decimals correspond to rational numbers whose reduced denominators have only factors 2 and 5.
  • Cross-cancel factors in multiplication only, never across addition terms.
  • A fraction a/(b+c) cannot be split into a/b+a/c.
  • When choosing smart numbers, avoid values that accidentally make two distinct answer expressions equal.
