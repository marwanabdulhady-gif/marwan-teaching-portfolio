Think Like a GMAT Test-Taker

Translate completion time into jobs per hour, or distance into speed times time.

Faster Methods

Add rates, not hours; use consistent units.

Worked example 1

Workers take 3 and 6 hours alone. Find combined time.

T1​=31​+61​=21​

Together they take 2 hours.

Worked example 2

Opposing trains travel at 40 and 50 km/h for 2 hours. Find separation.

(40+50)⋅2=180

Their relative speeds add.

Worked example 3

A car travels 60 km at 30 km/h and returns 60 km at 60 km/h. Find overall average speed.

60/30+60/60120​=40

Do not take the simple mean of unequal travel times.

Worked example 4

One pipe fills a tank in 4 h and a drain empties it in 12 h. Find the net fill time.

T1​=41​−121​=61​

The drain subtracts from the net work rate, so T=6 h.

Worked example 5

A car travels 120 km at 60 km/h, rests for 30 minutes, then travels another 120 km at 80 km/h. What is its average speed for the entire trip, including the rest?

vavg​=120/60+0.5+120/80240​=60 km/h

Average speed uses total distance divided by total elapsed time. Include the 0.5-hour rest: 240 km in 4 hours.

Worked example 6

Pump A fills a tank in 4 hours and Pump B in 6 hours. Both run for 1 hour, then A is turned off. How long after the start is the tank full?

41​+61​=125​;1−125​=127​;1/67/12​=27​

Together the pumps fill 5/12 of the tank in the first hour. Pump B then needs 3.5 more hours to fill the remaining 7/12, so total time is 4.5 hours.

Common Traps

  • The combined completion time must be less than the fastest solo time.
