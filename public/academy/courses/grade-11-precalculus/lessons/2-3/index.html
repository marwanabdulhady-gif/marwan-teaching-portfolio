Grade 11 Precalculus · Lesson 2.3

Getting Information from the Graph

Read a graph as mathematical evidence: scan its axes, project points, identify intervals, compare functions, and justify every conclusion.

01 · Explore

Read the Graph, Not Just the Equation

⌄

Move the scanner along the graph. Read the input on the horizontal axis and the output on the vertical axis. A missing point is not in the domain.

Explanation

Mr. Marwan's method · Read a coordinate

  1. Read the horizontal scale and vertical scale separately.
  2. Start at the given x-value and project vertically until you meet the graph.
  3. Project horizontally to the y-axis and read the output.
  4. If the point is open, that endpoint is not included.
Worked example

Worked example · Reading the graph

On the first graph, the filled point (−4,3) gives f(−4)=3. The point (2,4) is open, so that piece does not define f(2).

Check the other piece: it starts at x=2 with an open point too, so f(2) is undefined.

02 · Project

Domain, Range & Intercepts

⌄

Domain: project the whole graph onto the x-axis. Range: project onto the y-axis. Check every open and closed endpoint before writing interval notation.

Explanation

Mr. Marwan's method · Project onto the axes

  1. For domain, shade every x-value under the graph; for range, shade every output height on the y-axis.
  2. Keep gaps as separate intervals connected by the union symbol.
  3. Use square brackets for included endpoints and parentheses for excluded endpoints.
  4. Write only the x-values for domain and only the y-values for range.
Worked example

Worked example · Disconnected graph

For the first lab graph, the left piece occupies x from −4 to 0 with filled endpoints, and the second occupies x greater than 2 up to 5 inclusive.

Domain: [−4,0] ∪ (2,5]. Range: [−1,3] ∪ 4.

03 · Scan

Function Values, Horizontal Lines & Net Change

⌄
Explanation

Mr. Marwan's method · Solve from a graph

  1. For f(a), go from x=a to the curve and read the height.
  2. For f(x)=k, draw a horizontal line y=k and record every intersection x-coordinate.
  3. For f(x)>k or f(x)≤k, find the segments above or below the line and transfer them to the x-axis.
  4. Check whether equality is included, and never confuse a point with an interval.
Worked example

Worked example · Four crossings at height zero

The graph below crosses the x-axis at x=−3, −1, 1 and 3. Hence f(x)=0 has four solutions, while f(x)≥0 includes the intervals [−3,−1] and [1,3].

04 · Compare

Two Functions on One Coordinate Plane

⌄
Explanation

Mr. Marwan's method · Compare two outputs

  1. Draw or inspect both functions on the same coordinate system.
  2. Intersections mean f(x)=g(x); read their x-coordinates.
  3. Where the f-graph is higher, f(x)>g(x); where the g-graph is higher, f(x)<g(x).
  4. Split intervals at each intersection and check strict or inclusive endpoints.
Worked example

Worked example · A line and a parabola

For f(x)=2x²+3 and g(x)=5x+6, the graphs intersect at x=−0.5 and x=3.

Between the crossings, the parabola is below the line, so f(x)≤g(x) for −0.5≤x≤3.

05 · Follow the Curve

Increasing, Decreasing & Constant

⌄
Explanation

Mr. Marwan's method · Follow the graph from left to right

  1. Read the x-axis from left to right; ignore whether the graph happens to be above or below zero.
  2. Going up means increasing, going down means decreasing, and a horizontal segment means constant.
  3. Use intervals of x-values, conventionally open intervals for increasing and decreasing.
  4. At a turning point, separate the adjoining intervals.
Interactive lab

Behavior of f(x) = x^(2/3)

Move the scanner and predict the turning behavior. For real inputs, x^(2/3) means the square of the real cube root.
How does the function behave as x passes through zero?
Print reference · Domain: all real numbers. Range: [0, ∞). Decreasing on (−∞,0), increasing on (0,∞); absolute minimum (0,0). The function is even. The displayed window is not the domain.

Read from left to right. Rising means increasing, falling means decreasing, and horizontal means constant. State the intervals in terms of x, typically using open intervals for increasing/decreasing behavior.

06 · Find the Peaks

Maximum and Minimum: Point vs Value

⌄

A maximum point gives both coordinates; the maximum value is only the y-coordinate. A local maximum is highest nearby, while an absolute maximum is highest on the entire domain.

Explanation

Mr. Marwan's method · Point versus value

  1. Find the peaks and valleys and read both coordinates carefully.
  2. Report the maximum or minimum point as (x,y); report its value as y only.
  3. Local means highest or lowest nearby; absolute means highest or lowest over the entire domain.
  4. Check endpoints and disconnected pieces before claiming an absolute extreme.
Worked example

Worked example · Quartic peaks and valleys

For f(x)=12x²+4x³−3x⁴, a local maximum point is (−1,5), a local minimum point is (0,0), and the absolute maximum point is (2,32).

The absolute maximum value is 32, not the coordinate pair (2,32).

07 · Apply

A Graph Tells a Story

⌄

Temperature, weight, water levels and traffic capacity: interpret the axes and units, describe increasing/decreasing intervals, locate extremes, and calculate net change in context.

Interactive lab

Real-World Graph Investigation · Reservoir

Read time in hours and depth in metres. Move the scanner, then choose a prediction for each interpretation task. The depth scale starts at 20 m, not zero.

Interpretation task 1 of 6

What is the reservoir depth at hour 4?
Print reference ·

What is the reservoir depth at hour 4? 60 m. Read the height at x=4.

What is the net change from hour 2 to hour 6? -30 m. Final minus initial: 30-60=-30 m.

When is the water level constant? (2,4). The graph is horizontal between hours 2 and 4.

Where is the water level decreasing? (4,6). The graph falls as time increases from 4 to 6.

What is the absolute maximum value? 60 m. The greatest height is 60; it is reached throughout [2,4].

What is the range of the water depth? [30,60] m. Every depth from 30 to 60 appears on connected line segments.

Key takeawayMr. Marwan Exam Tip

Always attach units to values, and use intervals of the independent variable when describing trends.
