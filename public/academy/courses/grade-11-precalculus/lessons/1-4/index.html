1. Rational Expressions & Domain Restrictions

⌄

A rational expression is a quotient of two polynomials. The denominator cannot equal zero. Record restrictions from the original denominator before cancelling.

Interactive worked example · restrictions survive cancellation
x2+5x+6x2+8x+15\dfrac{x^2+5x+6}{x^2+8x+15}x2+8x+15x2+5x+6​

Record restrictions from the original denominator before cancelling anything.

Your turn · Factor the original denominator

Work this step on paper first. The solution stays hidden until you confirm you are ready.

1. Factor the original denominator

x2+8x+15=(x+3)(x+5)x^2+8x+15=(x+3)(x+5)x2+8x+15=(x+3)(x+5)

2. State restrictions

x≠−3,−5x\ne-3,-5x=−3,−5

3. Factor the numerator

x2+5x+6=(x+2)(x+3)x^2+5x+6=(x+2)(x+3)x2+5x+6=(x+2)(x+3)

4. Cancel factors only

x+2x+5,x≠−3,−5\dfrac{x+2}{x+5},\quad x\ne-3,-5x+5x+2​,x=−3,−5

Routine: Factor completely → state restrictions → cancel common factors → simplify.

Mr. Marwan's preferred method

8. Alternative Method · Simplify Then Divide

⌄

This method is valid, but it is the secondary method in this lesson. First simplify the large numerator into one fraction, simplify the large denominator into one fraction, then multiply by the reciprocal.
