⌄

02 · Read the inside and outside

HSRV — Your Transformation Roadmap

⌄

Use the HSRV framework: Horizontal shifting, Stretching/shrinking, Reflecting, Vertical shifting. Each can be horizontal or vertical where applicable.

Explanation

Mr. Marwan's clue · Outside: keep it · Inside: inverse it

Outside the function, operations change y-values directly. Inside the function, operations change x-values inversely. A minus sign outside reflects across the x-axis; a minus sign inside reflects across the y-axis.

g(x)=af(bx−h)+k,b≠0g(x)=a f(bx-h)+k,\qquad b\ne0g(x)=af(bx−h)+k,b=0(x,y)↦(x+hb,  ay+k)(x,y)\mapsto\left(\frac{x+h}{b},\;ay+k\right)(x,y)↦(bx+h​,ay+k)

Solve the inside equation for the new input: bX−h=xbX-h=xbX−h=x. In this unfactored form, the horizontal shift is H=h/bH=h/bH=h/b, not generally hhh.

bx−h=b(x−H),H=hbbx-h=b(x-H),\qquad H=\frac{h}{b}bx−h=b(x−H),H=bh​

The equivalent factored form is af(b(x−H))+ka f(b(x-H))+kaf(b(x−H))+k, with point map (x,y)↦(H+x/b,ay+k)(x,y)\mapsto(H+x/b,ay+k)(x,y)↦(H+x/b,ay+k). The lab uses its hhh control in bx−hbx-hbx−h, so the actual horizontal shift is H=h/bH=h/bH=h/b when b≠0b\ne0b=0.

For a transformed domain, solve bx−h∈Dfbx-h\in D_fbx−h∈Df​. When dividing an inequality by a negative bbb, reverse its direction.

What Does the Equation Tell You?

⌄
Worked example

Worked example · Describe the combined transformation

y=∣x+3∣−6y=|x+3|-6y=∣x+3∣−6 comes from y=∣x∣y=|x|y=∣x∣: 3 units left and 6 units down. The vertex moves from (0,0) to (−3,−6).

  • y = |x|
  • y = |x + 3| − 6
Worked example

Worked example · Write the equation after a shift

Starting with y=x2y=x^2y=x2, shift 7 units right and 2 units down: y=(x−7)2−2y=(x-7)^2-2y=(x−7)2−2.

  • y = x²
  • y = (x − 7)² − 2
Worked example

Worked example · Distinguish stretching from shifting

y=x+2+2y=\sqrt{x+2}+2y=x+2​+2 shifts left 2 and up 2. But y=2xy=2\sqrt{x}y=2x​ stretches vertically by a factor of 2; its endpoint remains (0,0).

  • y = √(x + 2) + 2
  • y = 2√x

07 · Symmetry connection

Even, Odd or Neither?

⌄

A graph is even if it is symmetric about the y-axis, and odd if it is symmetric about the origin. The domain must also support the required symmetry.

f(−x)=f(x)evenf(-x)=f(x)\quad\text{even}f(−x)=f(x)evenf(−x)=−f(x)oddf(-x)=-f(x)\quad\text{odd}f(−x)=−f(x)odd
Explanation

Mr. Marwan's clue · Substitute −x first

Replace every x by (−x), simplify powers and signs, then compare with the original f(x) and its negative −f(x). Never decide solely from a single plotted point.

08 · Check every transformation

Exam Strategy and Mastery

⌄
Key takeaway

The Mr. Marwan checklist

  1. Recognize the parent and mark guide points.
  2. Read inside versus outside correctly.
  3. Follow HSRV and write the image of every point.
  4. Plot the final graph with an accurate coordinate scale.
  5. Check domain, range, endpoints and asymptotes after transformation.
  6. For symmetry, verify f(−x) algebraically.
